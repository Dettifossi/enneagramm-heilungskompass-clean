=== PORTRAIT: Lena Meyer-Landrut ===
[SUBTITLE] Singer, ESC winner 2010, b. 1991 – Animal equivalent: Peacock
[H2] 1. The Peacock: The Most Beautiful Animal in the World – Even If It Has No Idea
[H2] 2. The Sexual Three: Personal Magnetism as a Natural Gift
[H2] 3. The Two Wing: Connection as a Matter of the Heart
[H2] 4. The Overall Impact: Eurovision, Music, and the Courage for Imperfection
[H2] 5. The Peacock: Radiance Without the Spotlight
[H2] 6. The Path to Healing: When Attraction Transforms into Authenticity
[P] The peacock is the animal of the sexual Three, and it does not display its plumage on command. It does so because it is its nature. What sometimes distinguishes it: it does not always know it itself. Lena Meyer-Landrut is this peacock in a very specific manifestation. She won the Eurovision Song Contest in 2010 with Satellite virtually out of nowhere, as an 18-year-old without a planned pop career, discovered by Stefan Raab at the Bundesvision Song Contest. She stood on stage like someone who was currently very surprised, yet simultaneously belonged right there.
[P] That is the paradox of the SX3: it attracts without seducing. It shines without staging. Lena's stage magnetism has nothing of a orchestrated glamour machine. She wears sneakers, makes jokes about herself, and the audience cannot stop watching her.
[P] The Two wing (w2) reinforces this: the SX3w2 does not only radiate—it also warms. This difference is instantly palpable. You don't look at Lena because you have to. You look because you feel seen.
[P] The sexual Three (SX3) is the subtype that uses attractiveness not as a strategy, but as a genuine expression of its nature. Naranjo called this subtype Masculinity / Femininity—the embodiment of attractiveness in its direct, personal form. Not the crowd, not status—but the person opposite, the moment, the connection.
[P] Lena possesses this energy in a very unusual manifestation: she seduces not through perfection, but through authenticity. Her performances, interviews, and social media moments are characterized by self-irony, humor, and a directness that other stars wouldn't dare to show. The SX3 doesn't need to look perfect to have an impact. It needs to be present. And Lena is always present.
[P] This makes her an SX3 who rewrites the core pattern of this energy: instead of "Look how I shine," the message is "I am really here, and so are you." This quality is rare and humanly precious.
[P] The Two wing (w2) brings something decisive to the Three energy: a genuine joy in connection. The Two doesn't just want to be admired—it wants to be connected. In Lena Meyer-Landrut, this shows in the way she interacts with fans: not as a brand to consumers, but as a human to humans.
[P] She has spoken openly about her mental health crises—about depression, exhaustion, the moments when fame was not enough. That is no marketing strategy. That is the Two wing: the SX3w2 shares because it feels that real connection only arises through truthfulness, and because it hopes to help others who feel alone.
[P] The SX3w2 doesn't just attract—it cares. This difference explains why Lena, after more than fifteen years in the spotlight, still has a fan base that takes her personally, not as a product.
[P] Lena Meyer-Landrut's career is unusual for an ESC winner: she hasn't faded away. Thirteen years after Satellite, she has an active fan base, releases music that gets heard, and is considered an authentic voice of her generation. That is not due to brilliant management—it is because she feels genuine.
[P] The SX3 survives in show business not through adaptation, but through personality. Lena's musical style has shifted from ESC pop to more experimental, darker sounds. In doing so, she follows not the market, but herself. That is the mature SX3: attraction through integrity, not through submission to trends.
[P] Her light and her shadow lie close together. The core pattern of the Three is deception—not as malice, but as a structural hazard: the self that begins to mistake itself for its effect. Lena's public engagement with this question—who am I when nobody is watching?—shows an SX3 who knows her shadow.
[P] What distinguishes the peacock of the sexual Three from other Three subtypes is the intimacy of its effect. The social Three shines for the audience. The sexual Three shines for you—specifically for you, in this moment. Lena Meyer-Landrut possesses this gift in a rare manifestation: in a concert with thousands, she can create the feeling that she is talking to you. Not to the crowd. To you.
[P] This quality arises not through technique, but through a form of attention that the SX3 possesses naturally: Who is the person in front of me? What do they need? How do I truly reach them? Lena's stage presence is direct, sometimes awkward, always alive. The peacock doesn't need to be perfect to enchant. It needs to be itself.
[P] The healing path of the Three leads from the question "How do I come across?" to the realization "I am independent of how I come across." For the SX3, this means: no longer needing to seduce, but being allowed to be connected. Attraction turns into authenticity. Charisma turns into character.
[P] Lena Meyer-Landrut has taken steps along this path in public: taking breaks, setting boundaries, speaking about inner exhaustion at a time when that was still a risk in show business, not a trend. That is the SX3w2 on her healing path: exchanging facade for depth, radiance for warmth, applause for genuine encounter.
[P] The peacock that stops unfolding its fan because it thinks it has to—and discovers that it is loved just as it is. That is the most beautiful moment of this transformation.

=== PORTRAIT: Linda Leinweber ===
[SUBTITLE] Psychologist, author, coach, b. 1988 – Animal equivalent: Chihuahua
[H2] 1. The Chihuahua That Learned to Look
[H2] 2. The Sexual Four: Rivalry Against Trivialization
[H2] 3. The Five Wing: The Analyst Behind the Voice
[H2] 4. Poise and the Vision of Prevention
[H2] 5. Light and Shadow: The Fire That Understands
[H2] 6. The Chihuahua Showing Others the Way
[P] The Chihuahua is the animal of the sexual Four: small in stature, vast in inner life, and equipped with an intensity that knows no middle ground. The Chihuahua loves deeply, feels sharper than most around it, and will not be intimidated—yet it is also sensitive, vulnerable, and dependent on connection like hardly any other animal. What it has built, it protects. What it has felt, it does not forget. And what it has once understood, it passes on.
[P] Linda-Marlen Leinweber is a psychologist, author, coach, and founder of the online platform Poise, as well as one of the most prominent German voices in the field of mental health and prevention. Her path there led not through theory, but through personal experience: through years in which she stood speechlessly facing what her body and psyche were telling her. This biography is no coincidence. It is the foundation.
[P] What distinguishes Linda Leinweber from many other psychologists and coaches is this connection: she does not speak about pain from the outside. She speaks from an inner place that knows this pain and has learned to read it. That is SX4w5: depth that feels no shame. Insight that does not hide.
[P] The sexual Four (SX4) is, in the Enneagram tradition, the subtype that does not carry the pain of the Four inward, but turns it outward—as intensity, as craving, as fire. Naranjo calls this subtype Rivalry: the burning conviction that others were given something withheld from oneself—and the drive to compete against it, to surpass, to prove oneself. In the SX4, this drive does not remain abstract. It directs itself at something concrete—at a person, a system, an injustice.
[P] In Linda Leinweber, this energy sought out a clear opponent: the trivialization of psychological distress. The notion that anxiety and panic can be overcome if you just pull yourself together. The idea that burnout is a matter of discipline rather than the system. That prevention is a luxury rather than common sense. Against this attitude—which she experienced not least in herself during years with panic attacks and exhaustion—she directs her work like a counterweight.
[P] Her own crisis was the turning point. Linda Leinweber was already working as a model and had studied psychology in Cologne and Berlin when she experienced firsthand what it means when the nervous system collapses: panic attacks, pressure, the feeling of sitting in a hamster wheel she had built herself. She didn't bury this experience. She made it the foundation of her entire professional trajectory. That is SX4: pain is not managed. It becomes the compass.
[P] The Five wing (w5) brings something decisive to the sexual Four: the analytical lens, the hunger for understanding, the ability not only to feel one's own experience, but to penetrate it. The SX4 alone could get stuck in the intensity of its own feelings; the Five wing pulls it into observation, into theory, into craft. It says: feeling is not enough—you also have to understand.
[P] Linda Leinweber's educational path bears this signature: psychology degree, systemic organizational consulting, certified coaching, hypnotherapy. That is not a collection of certificates—that is a Five wing moving from one tool to the next because no single one is sufficient. Hypnotherapy stands out in particular: it engages where classical talk therapy hits its limits—in the deep subconscious, in body memory, in patterns that cannot be dissolved by insight alone. Anyone taking this path thinks in depths.
[P] The Five wing also manifests in Leinweber's publishing work. Her book Frei und trotzdem verbunden ("Free yet connected")—a title that itself contemplates a paradox—is dedicated to relationship dynamics, attachment patterns, and conflict resolution. This is no classic self-help book: no "10 steps to a better relationship," but an attempt to understand why people get stuck in the exact same patterns and what it takes to find a way out. That is SX4w5 in its best element: depth that serves others.
[P] With Poise, Linda Leinweber created a platform that anchors her approach institutionally: an interdisciplinary team of psychologists, psychotherapists, and coaches making psychological counseling and support accessible online. The name is no coincidence—poise means balance, posture, inner stability. Not drama. Not spectacular healing. But: the measure that sustains. Digging a bit deeper reveals: the English word poise and the English word poison share the same root—the Old French poison originally meant drink, potion, poison. The name thus carries both within itself: balance and antidote. And when one knows that the unconscious SX4 can carry a certain toxic quality in its gaze—an intensity that burns, a rivalry that gnaws, a passion that flips—the platform name gains a second interpretation: Poise as a consciously chosen counter-image to one's own shadow. Balance as the answer to that which can derail.
[P] The preventive approach is central here: Leinweber aims to reach people before the crisis hits. Before burnout arrives, before panic disorder establishes itself, before the relationship breaks apart. That is a deeply systemic thought, explaining why she is also active in corporate consulting: B2B workshops, mental health concepts for executives, structural prevention instead of individual firefighting. The Chihuahua that doesn't wait until the house is ablaze.
[P] Through her Instagram channel (@psychologin_lindaleinweber) and her podcast Das Wunder Frau, she reaches a broad audience beyond the clinical setting. This commitment to destigmatization—the message that mental health is not a luxury topic, but a fundamental question of life—is her most consistent political stance. Not in parliament, but where attitudes are formed: in people's everyday lives.
[P] The core pattern of the Four is envy—the deep, often unconscious feeling that others were gifted a completeness missing in oneself. In the SX4, this envy wears a more personal face: not glancing at others' possessions, but the sting when someone else is allowed to effortlessly be what one finds so difficult to be. Light, free, unburdened. The SX4 knows the feeling of being heavier than others—and has found ways to work with it.
[P] The light of the SX4w5 lies in its ability to render its own darkness productive—not as a performance, but as genuine service. Linda Leinweber doesn't help because she ought to. She helps because she knows what it feels like when nobody helped. This difference is palpable. People who help from personal experience pass something along differently than those who have merely learned how cognitively.
[P] The shadow: the Five wing tends toward withdrawal, over-processing thoughts, holding back until everything is truly thought through. The SX4, on the other hand, presses for connection, for resonance, for immediate contact. This inner tension between analysis and expression, between depth and contact, is the most productive thing the SX4w5 combination generates. And at times, the most exhausting.
[P] The healing path of the Four leads from the question "Why am I not like the others?" to the realization "What I am is unique, and it is mine." For the SX4w5, this means not tempering one's own intensity, but directing it—not against oneself, not against the world, but toward what is possible.
[P] Linda Leinweber walked this path in her biography. From experiencing panic attacks herself to a grounded education, from education to the platform, from the platform to a public voice for mental health. That is no self-optimization project. That is a Four that learned not to hide her inner world, but to carry it—and transformed that carrying into a craft that serves others.
[P] The Chihuahua from Altmünster on Lake Traunsee is not afraid of big feelings. It learned to read them. That is the sexual Four with a Five wing. That is Linda Leinweber.

=== PORTRAIT: Mariah Carey ===
[SUBTITLE] Singer, songwriter, producer, b. 1969 in Huntington, New York
[H2] 1. The Gorilla
[H2] 2. The Family: Abundance as Home
[H2] 3. The Eight Wing: Power as a Shield
[H2] 4. The Work: Five Octaves for Eternity
[H2] 5. Light and Shadow: The Diva and the Child
[H2] 6. The Gorilla That Owns Christmas
[P] The gorilla is the strongest animal of the rainforest and the warmest. It lives in a tight family bond, nurtures its relationships, protects its own with a power it rarely needs to display. It doesn't need to seek out a fight; its presence alone suffices. And it loves food not as a weakness, but as an expression of life, joy, abundance. The gorilla is generous with what it has. And it never forgets who harmed it.
[P] Mariah Carey is the best-selling female artist of all time—or at least one of the contenders for that title, depending on how you count. She has eighteen number-one singles on the US Billboard Hot 100—more than any other solo artist in the chart's history. Her voice spans five octaves, including the whistle register she was the first to popularize. And she wrote a single Christmas song—All I Want for Christmas Is You (1994)—that continues to bring her several million dollars annually. Mariah Carey is the gorilla: warm, powerful, lavish, and fully prepared to look those who stand in her way straight in the eye.
[P] Claudio Naranjo called the self-preservation Type 7 Family because this subtype does not seek security in the wide world, but in the tightest circle. It builds nests: out of familiarity, warmth, enjoyment. And it gathers experiences, people, pleasures, security. Abundance for the SE7 is not excess; it is proof that life is good and will remain good.
[P] Carey grew up in precarious circumstances: her parents separated when she was three years old, and she moved from place to place with her mother. Her mother, Patricia Hickey, was an Irish-American opera singer and vocal coach; her father, Alfred Roy Carey, was of Venezuelan-African American origin and an aeronautical engineer. Mariah grew up between two worlds—too Black for her mother's white environment, too light for her father's Black environment—and learned early on to make herself her own home. At eighteen, she moved to New York City, waitressed, styled hair, and worked tirelessly toward her breakthrough.
[P] When she finally built her nest, she built it big: penthouses, private jets, butterflies as decor, champagne on the return flight. Her fans—the Lambs—are not just an audience, but a family. She communicates with them directly and keeps them in her inner circle. That is the SE7: the nest is never big enough, and whoever belongs to it truly belongs.
[P] The Eight wing gives the self-preservation Seven a quality the pure SE7 does not always show: the will to power, readiness for confrontation, and a keen radar for control and its loss. Where the SE7 seeks joy and avoids discomfort, the Eight wing adds: "And if someone threatens my joy, I will let them know."
[P] The defining experience of Carey's life was her marriage to music executive Tommy Mottola (1993–1998), the then-head of Sony Music. Mottola had discovered her, built her up, and then controlled her: her home was guarded, her phone calls monitored, her circle subject to approval. Carey later described it as a kind of golden cage. When she freed herself, she wrote Fantasy, Emotions, and ultimately the album Butterfly (1997)—an R&B pivot Mottola would never have approved. The Eight wing taught her: loss of control is more dangerous than the big performance. And: whoever has once lost control never surrenders it voluntarily again.
[P] The diva legends that have developed since then—the butterflies in the room, the specific drinking water, the famous line "I don't know her" when asked about Jennifer Lopez—are no coincidence. They are a protective wall. "Whoever doesn't respect me doesn't exist for me." That is the Eight wing of the gorilla: clear boundaries.
[P] Mariah Carey's debut album Mariah Carey (1990) produced five consecutive number-one singles in the US—a record previously held only by Whitney Houston. It was followed by Emotions (1991), Music Box (1993), Merry Christmas (1994)—the best-selling Christmas album of all time to this day—Daydream (1995), and Butterfly (1997). In that decade, she was simply the biggest pop voice in the world.
[P] In 2001, she collapsed publicly. The album Glitter and the movie of the same name flopped catastrophically; Carey suffered a public nervous breakdown documented in live appearances and a tearful, seemingly incoherent visit to Total Request Live. She disappeared from the public eye and received inpatient treatment. It was the lowest point of her career and simultaneously the beginning of the most important work she ever did.
[P] The Emancipation of Mimi (2005) was one of the greatest comebacks in pop history. The album went fifteen times platinum in the US; the single We Belong Together was the most played song of the decade in America. Carey didn't just return—she ascended higher than ever before. That is the SE7w8: you don't defeat the gorilla. You only make it stronger.
[P] The light of the SE7w8 is its capacity for joy. Mariah Carey laughs loudly, loves opulently, gives generously. Her memoir The Meaning of Mariah Carey (2020) is no self-pity project, but an honest, often humorous look at a life full of ruptures and ascents. She sugarcoats nothing and apologizes for nothing. The gorilla principle: I was here. I survived. Here is my story.
[P] The shadow: the SE7 avoids what hurts. Carey acted publicly for years as if she had no bad days—as if the diva persona were not a mask, but reality itself. The Eight wing reinforces this: showing weakness is dangerous. The result is images that are sometimes more self-protection than self-expression. The famous rider clauses, the extravagances, the distance from anything uncontrollable—that is not just diva behavior. That is a human who once fell too deep into a world without control and will never allow that to happen again.
[P] Her children—twins Moroccan and Monroe, born in 2011 with Nick Cannon—are the one thing for which the diva pose drops. In interviews about them, Carey appears unfiltered, warm, fully present. That is the gorilla without the protective wall: the mother in the nest.
[P] Mariah Carey owns Christmas. That is no metaphor. All I Want for Christmas Is You (1994) now has over a billion streams on Spotify alone, and the song returns to the charts every year as if it had a right to be there. It does. Carey wrote it, by her own account, in fifteen minutes on a piano one afternoon.
[P] Five octaves, thirty-five years in the spotlight, eighteen number-one singles, a public breakdown, a comeback that changed history, and an immortal Christmas song: that is the gorilla. It builds its nest. It protects its own. It sings—and when it sings, the world listens.
